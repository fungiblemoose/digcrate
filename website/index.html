Download on the Mac App Store

Plan DJ sets that mix in key.

DigCrate reads your local library for BPM, musical key, and energy, then helps you build sets with clean harmonic transitions and export playlists for your DJ software.

It is a Mac app, and the audio analysis runs on your Mac. You can plan with Apple’s on-device intelligence or with a model server you configure. No DigCrate account is required.

Analyze your library on your Mac

Point DigCrate at a folder and it reads the BPM, the musical key in 8A-style harmonic notation, and the energy of every track. All of it happens on your Mac.

Tracks with shaky analysis are flagged in a review queue. Fix a BPM or key by hand, confirm the rest, and keep your library clean.

Audition any track from a smart cue point that drops you near the part that matters, instead of the silence at the top. Filter by BPM, key, or energy, run a text search, and see the whole library at a glance, with counts, ranges, and most-common keys.

Describe the set, then review the plan

Describe the set in plain English, such as “60-minute liquid roller, mellow start, peak around minute 40,” and the planner picks and orders tracks from your library for flow.

Every handoff is scored on the harmonic wheel, with a plain-language note on why it works or why it clashes. Listen through the proposed transitions, and reorder tracks or fill a gap before you export. Harmonic scores are a planning aid; your ears make the final decision.

Find the weak transitions and fill them

Weak transitions are flagged with the BPM, key, and energy of the bridge track that would smooth them over. One click opens Beatport, Spotify, or SoundCloud searches built from the gap’s profile, so you know exactly what to hunt for. Those searches open in your browser.

Export to Rekordbox XML or M3U

Export a finished set as Rekordbox XML or an M3U playlist with metadata intact, ready to review and import into compatible DJ software.

What your Mac needs

DigCrate runs on Apple Silicon with macOS 14 or later. Apple’s on-device language-model planning requires macOS 26 or later with Apple Intelligence enabled and available. A model server you configure is another option; without an available language model, the planner can fall back to rules-based ordering.

Library analysis is local. A configured model server receives your planning prompt and selected track metadata, so use a server you trust. The current 1.x release does not include Apple Cloud planning. Read the privacy details.
